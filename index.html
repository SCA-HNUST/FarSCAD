Overview

This repository releases Far-field Side-Channel Attack Dataset (FarSCAD), a large-scale dataset designed to support the study and evaluation of screaming-channel attacks across multiple cryptographic implementations, acquisition distances, noise conditions, and trace desynchronization levels. FarSCAD contains more than 3M traces collected from three representative cryptographic implementations, including TinyAES, SM4, and CRYSTALS-Kyber, running on seven physically distinct nRF52 DK boards. To characterize the impact of remote acquisition distance, the dataset covers seven acquisition distances, ranging from a cable-connected reference measurement to far-field measurements at 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, and 30 m. In addition, FarSCAD incorporates three noise conditions, which are low, mid, and high, as well as three desynchronization levels, which are zero, mid, and high.

The experimental platform uses the nRF52 DK as the target device. Far-field EM Side-Channel leakage is captured using an Ettus Research N210 USRP, equipped with an Ettus XBS-400 RF daughterboard and a TL-ANT2424B grid parabolic antenna for directional long-distance acquisition.

Implementation

Hardware setup

The transmitter is an nRF52 Bluetooth SoC running AES, SM4, or Kyber. The receiver uses an ANT2424B parabolic antenna, connected through an SMA cable to an Ettus N210 SDR with an Ettus SBX daughterboard.

Experimental environments

The experimental environment includes a corridor deployment with spatially separated target and receiver. The figure shows the environment model, capture devices, and target setup.
